## Rubrics
Mapping confidence rubric (0-5 integer). Applies to both the overall mapping confidence AND to each affected_items[*].match_confidence. Match_confidence floor for emission is 3 — candidates that would score 2 or below are omitted entirely rather than hedged. Over-confidence is a worse failure than under-confidence.

- 5: Unambiguous — the change explicitly names the item or an identifier the item carries (e.g., the change text mentions "Form X-17A-5" and the candidate is the Form X-17A-5 filing obligation).
- 4: Strongly supported — the change targets a parameter / scope / content that the item directly covers, one short inferential step from the change text (e.g., change tightens a filing deadline, candidate obligation IS that filing).
- 3: Reasonable — the item is substantively affected by the change but a reviewer might legitimately disagree about scope (e.g., change affects 'DER aggregation operations', candidate project is a specific DER aggregation program).
- 2: Weak — topically adjacent but the match depends on an inference the change text does not clearly support. Below the floor for emission: omit the candidate entirely rather than emitting a match_confidence=2 hedge.
- 1: Speculative — surface keyword overlap with no substantive effect. Below the floor for emission.
- 0: Guess — no defensible overlap. Below the floor for emission; escalate if uncertain.

Recommended-action rubric. Pick exactly one.

- no_op: Nothing in the candidate set is substantively affected. The change is topically relevant but no obligation, project, or document in the company context requires any update. Choose when match_confidence on every candidate would be 2 or lower (and those matches are therefore omitted entirely).
- review: At least one candidate is substantively affected AND a first-pass human read is needed to decide WHAT to update. Choose when the affected item is clear but the required edit is a judgment call (e.g., 'tariff needs an update but Rachel needs to decide which sections').
- amend: At least one candidate is substantively affected AND there is a known concrete update (e.g., add a new required field to a form template, edit a specific tariff section, update an obligation parameter). Choose when the required edit is directly implied by the change text itself. When uncertain between review and amend, prefer review.

Routing-destination glossary. Pick the single PRIMARY team accountable for the change (multi-destination routing is a known v2 extension — TDD §8.6).

- market_ops: Market participation and operational workflows. Owns changes that affect wholesale market bidding, dispatch, settlement calendars, size-eligibility thresholds, participation registration, and day-ahead / real-time operational cadence. Does NOT own external regulatory filings or policy documents.
- legal: External regulatory submissions and policy. Owns changes that affect tariff filings, compliance plans, external forms (SEC, FERC, state PUC), external training requirements, and policy documents. Does NOT own operational workflows or metering.
- it_metering: Telemetry, metering, and operational data pipelines. Owns changes that affect data feeds, meter configuration, telemetry cadence, data-element specifications in operational submissions, and the underlying systems that produce them. Does NOT own commercial market operations or external filings.

## Pitfalls
- Do NOT map an item just because its name is topically adjacent to the change. "DER" appearing in both the change and the item title is a surface overlap, not a substantive effect. Ask: does the change require a concrete update to THIS item's compliance parameters / content / scope? If no, omit it. See the obligation [1] / [2] mismatches in Example 3.
- Do NOT emit low-confidence match_confidence values to be safe. If match_confidence would be 2 or below, the item is not substantively affected — omit it entirely rather than including a hedge. Rachel's queue is noisier for every low-confidence match that ships; only emit matches you would defend.
- Do NOT use HEDGE VERBS in match_rationale. If your rationale needs "may require", "should reflect", "likely impacts", "could affect", or any other hedge, the item is below the emission floor — omit it. A match worth emitting at match_confidence=3 states a SPECIFIC overlap in the form "the change requires X; the item specifies X" — not "the change may affect how X is handled." The hedge verb is the signal that you are reaching for topical adjacency rather than naming substantive effect.
- Do NOT pick proposed_action="amend" on an item unless there is a known concrete edit implied for THAT item (new required field in this template, specific parameter edit on this obligation). "amend" means "we know what to change on this item"; "review" means "we need to look at this item first"; "no_op" means "this item is substantively affected but requires no edit." When uncertain between review and amend on a given item, prefer review.
- Do NOT emit all affected items with the SAME proposed_route when the Change touches multiple teams' domains. A tariff edit that also tightens a market-eligibility threshold produces at least two routes: legal for the tariff document, market_ops for the eligibility obligation. Routing by item ownership is what makes per-pair curation work in Rachel's queue — collapsing everything to one route defeats the point.
