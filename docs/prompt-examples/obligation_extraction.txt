## Rubrics
Obligation taxonomy. Every ExtractedObligation.obligation_type must be one of the four values below. The description + example calibrate what each value covers.

Perspective rule. Strata interprets every regulatory change from the REGULATED ENTITY's perspective — the aggregator, utility, hospital, broker, etc. whose compliance burden the system tracks. When a rule changes, the question is ALWAYS "does the regulated entity now have to do more, less, or the same?" — NOT "does the regulator have more or less authority?"

A regulator-side relaxation that creates de facto new burden on the regulated entity is a TIGHTENING of the entity's obligation. A regulator-side expansion that lifts a burden the entity previously faced is a LOOSENING. If the direction is still ambiguous after applying this rule, drop confidence to 3 and name the ambiguity in confidence_rationale.

- reporting: A submission the regulated entity must file with the regulator on a defined cadence or triggered event. Produces an artefact.
  Example: Monthly Renewable Portfolio Standard compliance filing to the state PUC listing retired RECs by month.
- operational: A continuous operating constraint the regulated entity must satisfy while running the regulated activity. Not a one-time submission.
  Example: Maintain real-time telemetry from aggregated DER resources to the RTO while those resources are participating in wholesale markets.
- procedural: A process the regulated entity must follow before, during, or after a triggered activity. Governs HOW an action is taken, not WHAT is reported.
  Example: Register each DER with the RTO at least 60 days before its first market participation.
- financial: An obligation that affects revenue, cost allocation, settlement, or market pricing — tariff provisions, charge formulas, settlement rules.
  Example: Aggregator pays MISO a $0.05/MWh settlement fee on cleared aggregation offers.

Confidence rubric (0-5 integer). Self-report your confidence in the conclusion AND set confidence_rationale naming which rubric level you picked and why. Over-confidence is a worse failure than under-confidence — when in doubt, drop one level.

- 5: Unambiguous — the cited passages state the conclusion verbatim.
- 4: Strongly supported — one short inferential step from the cited passages.
- 3: Reasonable — the cited passages support the conclusion but a reviewer might legitimately disagree.
- 2: Weak — surface signals point this way but the inference is thin.
- 1: Speculative — judgement call with limited anchor in the cited text.
- 0: Guess — no defensible anchor in the cited text; escalate.

## Pitfalls
- Do NOT extract a REGULATOR-side duty as an obligation of the regulated entity. "The Commission shall...", "FinCEN shall...", "The RTO/ISO shall accept bids from aggregators..." — these establish duties FOR THE REGULATOR, not for the regulated entity. Strata tracks the regulated entity's compliance set; regulator-side duties are entity RIGHTS, not entity obligations, and must be omitted. See Example 4 and the perspective rule in the Rubrics preamble.
- Do NOT read a sentence whose SUBJECT is a PROCESS as an obligation of the regulated entity just because the process name surface-mentions the entity ("the aggregator registration process shall not include...", "the broker-dealer onboarding process shall..."). Ask who ADMINISTERS the process; if the regulator (RTO / ISO / SEC / FinCEN / the Commission) runs it, the sentence is a regulator-side constraint on the regulator's rulemaking — see Example 5.
- Do NOT split one duty with multiple sub-elements into multiple obligations. "shall limit uses and disclosures of PHI to the minimum necessary" is ONE duty (apply the minimum-necessary standard to both uses and disclosures); the conjunction describes the scope of the duty, not two separate duties.
- Do NOT extract a "The X shall include Y, Z" sentence as a separate obligation when it FOLLOWS an "A shall file / register / produce X" sentence. The content-specification sentence has no independent compliance surface — violating it is violating the SAME primary duty. Combine into ONE obligation whose citing_span is the primary verb clause. See Example 6.
- Do NOT merge two independent duties into one. If two duties joined by "and shall ..." have separate compliance surfaces (one is a continuous constraint, the other produces an artefact), extract two obligations — see Example 2.
- Do NOT include surrounding modifiers in citing_span. Pick the SHORTEST span that identifies the duty — see Example 3.
- Do NOT emit confidence=5 on type-boundary cases. "shall maintain" could be operational (continuous constraint) or procedural (process); when the type is a judgment call, drop confidence to 3 or 4 and name the ambiguity in confidence_rationale.
