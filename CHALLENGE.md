The Challenge: Strata

A citation-grade regulatory intelligence and operations workspace for regulated enterprises

The Opportunity
Regulated enterprises need to turn regulatory change - dockets, filings, orders - into living projects tied to their own strategy, obligations, and documents. The hard part is not detecting that something changed, but understanding what changed, whether it is draft or final, what it affects, who must review it, and what action follows. The wedge is a trusted change-to-action workspace, not another search tool or alert feed.

Your Build
Build a regulatory change-intelligence workflow that:

Ingests successive versions of a regulatory proceeding plus a synthetic company context of obligations, projects, and documents.
Detects material changes between versions, distinguishes draft from final, and cites the exact source passages behind every claim.
Maps each change to the specific obligations, projects, and documents it affects and recommends an action with reviewer routing.
Maintains a living, auditable project state and escalates low-confidence interpretations for expert review instead of guessing.
Depth area: Go deep in at least one area: retrieval and citation grounding, document diffing at scale, obligation-extraction evals, reviewer workflow and routing, multi-jurisdiction taxonomy, or audit and rollback architecture.

How We Evaluate
Working prototype: This is the main event. Source code is required and the implementation must run: include the exact command (or live URL) that runs it end to end, and the exact command that runs your tests — we execute both before scheduling a panel.
Build quality: Rough is fine, and you may use any tools or AI assistants. A polished prompt, generated document, or thin LLM wrapper is not enough.
Review order: Reviewers start with the PRD and TDD, then verify the implementation through the prototype and source code.
PRD
Define the first regulatory affairs user, why alerts and search are insufficient, the first change-to-action wedge workflow, trust and adoption metrics for expert users, and expansion beyond utilities.

TDD
Cover ingestion, version diffing, evidence-linked extraction, the company-context data model, citation verification, confidence and escalation logic, reviewer routing, audit history and rollback, evals, data isolation, and security.
